# Motion Style Bible

Choose one lead style and no more than two accents. These recipes describe original art direction rather than copying existing videos.

## 1. Editorial kinetic typography
For essays, urgent claims and social issues. Oversized condensed/grotesk type, asymmetric grid, paper/ink contrast and one signal accent. Animate phrases with masks, tracking compression, stagger and meaningful type-to-shape morphs. Use tactile sparse sound, silence before reveals and readable holds. Avoid random glitch and constant jitter.

## 2. Data journalism and infographics
For statistics, policy and comparisons. Chart-first hierarchy, explicit units, consistent semantic colors, source notes and tabular numerals. Reveal comparisons progressively, grow marks from correct baselines and leave time to read. Avoid misleading axes, false precision and decorative numbers.

## 3. Scientific 2D/2.5D
For physics, biology and mechanisms. Layered cutaways, exploded views, transparent planes and functional color roles. Trace relevant paths, reveal cross-sections and return to context. Use controlled parallax and precise labels to explain cause/process/effect. Do not invent mechanisms or present particles as evidence.

## 4. Cinematic documentary / editorial titles
For history, journalism, profiles and technology. Disciplined editorial type, limited base palette, one motivated accent and layered depth. Reveal a detail, widen to context, use map/artifact match cuts and deliberate parallax. Build atmosphere sparingly. Avoid generic prestige tropes and meaningless grain.

## 5. Tactile / archival collage
For culture, memory, identity and history. Layer verified archival material, paper-like forms, annotations and restrained ink textures. Animate cutouts, masks, registration and parallax. Preserve evidence legibility, label reconstructions honestly and never fabricate historical documents.

## 6. Premium 3D / technology product
For devices and industrial mechanisms. Stable geometry, controlled reflections, macro detail, negative space and composited labels. Use plausible orbit, part separation, motivated light sweeps and a settled hero state. Sound should suggest material and weight. Avoid melted labels, unstable geometry and invented claims.

## 7. Character-led 2D / shape animation
For approachable education and human-scale metaphors. Clear silhouettes, stable rigs/props and limited expressive palette. Use anticipation, clear action poses, follow-through and controlled squash/stretch. Tie Foley to gestures. Avoid inconsistent proportions or motion that obscures the idea.

## 8. Swiss / modernist information design
For process explainers and structured lessons. Strong grid, alignment, controlled asymmetry, clean sans and a small semantic palette. Reveal modules through connection and reconfiguration. Avoid lifeless slide-deck movement and repeating the same card layout.

## 9. Generative / procedural abstraction
For networks, mathematics, music and systems. Use seeded deterministic particle/field/path rules, sparse labels and focal anchors. Build complexity, then simplify into insight. Sound follows meaningful state change. Avoid visual noise without explanatory purpose.

## 10. Maps / timelines / history
For routes, migration, chronology and historical analysis. Persistent map/timeline, readable dates and sourced boundaries. Animate route tracing, timeline scrubs and orientation-aware camera moves. Avoid unsourced borders or movement that implies unsupported causality.

## 11. Vertical / social-native explainer
For 9:16 mobile stories. Immediate hook, one idea per frame, oversized type, safe zones and strong contrast. Alternate fast reveals with readable holds. Avoid tiny details, interface collisions and random zooms.

## 12. Live action with graphic annotations
For journalism, field science and interviews. Real footage remains the evidence anchor; add tracked labels, masks, routes and transitions to simplified diagrams. Preserve context and room tone. Avoid untracked overlays and invented measurements.

## Selection protocol
Score candidates from 0–5 for topic fit, audience clarity, emotion, distinctiveness, feasibility and cost. Pick the best lead style and up to two accents. Trend coverage is hypothesis-generating, not universal proof. See `research/REFERENCE_SEED_CATALOG.md`.
