# Reference Pattern Matrix: 2026 Motion Graphics

Captured 2026-10-27. This document translates a small set of current public industry reports into production hypotheses. It is not a claim that this project has watched 269 videos, and it does not replace the planned 1,000-reference study.

## Secondary research with reported samples

### 1. UI / software animation: 100 videos
Source: [ADVIDS Research, “We Analyzed 100 UI Animation Videos”](https://advids.co/blog/ui-animation-motion-design-trends), published 12 September 2026 and updated 17 October 2026. The article says it coded 100 videos from 92 companies frame by frame, comparing 60 Q1/early-2026 videos with 40 from April through August 2026. Its downloadable CSV/JSON links currently return HTTP 410, so this project reviewed the article findings rather than the raw row-level dataset.

The report states:
- Product-screen-first construction rose from 22/60 (37%) to 25/40 (63%).
- Videos built from drawn graphics fell from 41/60 to 19/40.
- 3D appeared in 14/60 to 5/40 videos, about 23% to 13%.
- Videos carried mainly by moving type fell from 28/60 (47%) to 14/40 (35%), while a few lines of kinetic text remained common.
- The 0:46–1:30 runtime band fell from 35/60 to 16/40, while the sample spread more toward shorter and longer videos.
- Videos mixing light and dark frames fell from 20/60 (33%) to 7/40 (18%); light-ground videos increased.

### 2. Mixed-media B2B tech demos: 74 videos
Source: [ADVIDS Research, “The Cinematic Report: 74 B2B Tech Demos”](https://advids.co/blog/cinematic-mixed-media-b2b-tech-demos), published 14 September 2026. The article reports coding 74 videos from 62 companies, with 666 sampled frames and 769 timed segments. Reported patterns include product-screen plus cinematic-picture combinations in 61/74 videos, live action or stock picture in 62%, 3D CGI in 35%, and voiceover in 73%. Among videos containing both picture and product screen, 69% show the picture first; 79% of videos with a screen let it take the full frame. The report says 57% give screens more runtime than the picture.

### 3. Enterprise AI product launches: 95 videos
Source: [ADVIDS Research, “95 Enterprise AI Launch Videos”](https://advids.co/blog/enterprise-ai-launch-video-trends-2026), published 11 September 2026. The article reports 95 launch videos from 78 companies, 855 sampled frames, and a comparison between 52 Q1 videos and 43 from April–August 2026. Reported changes include live action decreasing from 19/52 to 7/43; captured product screens increasing from 12/52 to 17/43 while animated-only interfaces fell from 25 to 12; kinetic-type segments increasing from 26/52 to 28/43 while title-card segments fell from 35 to 24; brand-logo closing frames increasing from 29/52 to 32/43; and light frames increasing while mixed light/dark videos fell. The report notes all seven Anthropic/OpenAI launches in the later period had no voiceover, which is a narrow subset and must not be generalized to all educational video.

These three studies are by the same publisher, use B2B/software-heavy samples, and may overlap in individual video references. Do **not** add 100 + 74 + 95 and describe them as 269 unique videos. The reports do not measure viewer outcomes or objectively rate craft quality. They are article-level secondary evidence, not this studio's full-video review.

## Recent multi-style explainer examples: article-level evidence only

Source: [Moonb, “Animated explainer videos: 12 examples across 8 styles”](https://www.moonb.io/blog/animated-explainer-video), published 24 September 2026. The article discusses twelve recent examples from brand channels. The references are cataloged as R056–R067. This is L1 evidence: the article’s explanations were reviewed, but the underlying videos have not been watched here. It is not an independent measurement of performance or a substitute for timestamped scene review.

| Reference | Style | Transferable hypothesis to test |
|---|---|---|
| R056, Deel Overview | 2D vector / software | Turn an abstract global service into a map first, then a readable interface. |
| R057, Coinbase “What Is Cryptocurrency” | 2D vector / comparison | Pair an unfamiliar concept with a familiar visual analogue and explicit contrasts. |
| R058, Klarna payment history | Character-led brand story | Let a visual conflict or character journey carry the history instead of listing milestones. |
| R059, Octopus Energy solar explainer | Educational character / hybrid | Use characters to stage a mechanism, with real footage when it adds physical context. |
| R060, Plaid “How Plaid Works” | Motion graphics / invisible system | Represent an invisible connection as people, endpoints and a legible bridge or flow. |
| R061, Spotify for Artists “Showcase” | Product UI / abstract accents | Simplify interface cards to their essential signal; decorative 3D accents should earn their place. |
| R062, Lemonade insurance explainer | Hand-drawn line art | Use one semantic accent to mark what matters rather than coloring every object equally. |
| R063, ASML “Unveiling High NA EUV” | 3D industrial visualization | Use depth and camera movement to explain inaccessible internal mechanisms and scale. |
| R064, Boston Scientific WATCHMAN FLX Pro | 3D medical product | Use a clear product silhouette, controlled lighting and technical labels for a device that cannot be filmed in use. |
| R065, Too Good To Go platform | Live-action / UI hybrid | Establish human context, then hand over to a full-frame workflow with palette continuity. |
| R066, Todoist “What Is Todoist?” | Doodles → product UI | Use a contrast between the messy problem and the organized interface as part of the argument. |
| R067, Attio “Introducing Ask Attio” | Kinetic typography | Reserve the largest typographic movement for a few important phrases and preserve readable holds. |

**Cautions:** these are secondary article observations. Before adopting a recipe as a validated rule, inspect the original video, record timestamps and scene transitions, and check counterexamples. The article’s advice that motion graphics should reveal a meaningful change is a useful review question, not proof that these examples all succeed at it.

## Transferable production rules

1. **Choose media by the idea.** Do not make every film 3D. For a software walkthrough, show the real interface when available; for inaccessible internals or abstract concepts, use clear diagrams or original animation.
2. **Use moving type with intent.** Kinetic typography can create emphasis, but the entire explanation should not default to animated words. Let visual demonstrations and causal diagrams do their work.
3. **Use a motivated handoff.** A real-world or cinematic opening can establish context, then hand off to a full-frame interface, diagram, map or data view. Do not force mixed media if a simpler visual world is clearer.
4. **Protect reading time.** Full-frame interfaces, charts and detailed labels need sufficient hold. Scene density and runtime should respond to audience and channel, not a single fixed duration.
5. **Keep a coherent ground.** Avoid changing light/dark backgrounds without narrative purpose. Derive the ground from product, subject, footage and legibility, not a trend template.
6. **Design narration and visuals together.** Voiceover helps explain complex systems; music-only can work for short, self-sufficient statements when on-screen copy is clear. Do not remove narration simply because a few AI launch videos do so.
7. **Mix media when the transition explains something.** Live action/stock, real interface, vector diagrams, 2D/3D and kinetic type can coexist under one style bible. Every medium needs a purpose, transition rule and continuity anchor.
8. **Do not confuse trend with quality.** The reports describe what was common in selected samples, not what audiences preferred. Evaluate story clarity, original art direction, precision, accessibility and technical delivery separately.
9. **Use native format decisions early.** Plan 16:9, 9:16 or square from the brief; do not treat vertical as a careless late crop.
10. **Keep a style-specific sound grammar.** Audio should support the message and interface/physical actions, not add a repeated whoosh to every cut.

## Trends reported by other industry sources

- [Motionlab's 2026 B2B motion-design overview](https://motion-lab.agency/en/blog/tendances-motion-design-2026) highlights AI as a production assistant, selective 2D/3D hybrids, organic minimalism and native vertical planning.
- [Envato's 2026 motion-design trend article](https://elements.envato.com/learn/motion-design-trends) discusses hybrid AI workflows, tactile craft, mixed media, code-driven/generative work, flexible platform formats, and story/emotion as central.

These are trade/agency viewpoints, not a comprehensive scientific census. Use them as hypotheses for style selection and test against actual examples.

## How this matrix should evolve

For each new batch, link each pattern to the relevant reference IDs, label direct observations versus inference, and add counterexamples. Promote a rule to “validated” only after varied creators, languages, genres and L2/L3 evidence support it. The current reference catalog holds 67 public video candidates: 55 L0 metadata-only entries and 12 L1 article-supported entries, with zero L2/L3 full-video reviews confirmed; the 1,000-reference study remains unfinished.
