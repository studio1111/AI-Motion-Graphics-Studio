# Motion Graphics Style Recipe Library

Use one lead recipe and at most two accents. Replace bracketed tokens with actual art direction. These recipes are original reusable grammars, not copies of any reference film.

## 1. Editorial kinetic typography
“Build the scene around [claim]. Overscale [display archetype], asymmetric grid, deliberate negative space, [base palette] and one [signal accent]. Animate semantic phrases with tracking compression, mask reveals, stagger and one meaningful type-to-shape morph. Land on a readable hold. Use tactile sparse sound and silence before [reveal]. No random glitch or constant jitter.”

## 2. Scientific mechanism / 2.5D
“Explain [mechanism] as cause → process → effect. Construct layered cutaways and planes; distinguish [system A] and [system B] with stable material/color tokens. Trace only the relevant path, reveal a cross-section, then return to context. Use controlled parallax and a stable camera. Labels remain deterministic and connected to correct objects. Do not invent scientific details.”

## 3. Data journalism
“Turn sourced [dataset/question] into a visual argument. Show explicit units, a correct baseline, stable category colors, clear labels and source notes. Reveal one comparison at a time and let values settle. The camera serves the data. No decorative numbers, unlabeled scales or false precision.”

## 4. Cinematic documentary
“Create a restrained visual world around [topic] using [verified source material/texture], [limited palette] and layered depth. Start with an revealing detail, widen to context, then connect evidence with a motivated match cut or map/timeline move. One deliberate camera move and one major reveal per beat. Sound builds atmosphere without burying the voice. Avoid empty trailer clichés.”

## 5. Tactile archival collage
“Compose [story beat] from original paper-like forms, licensed archival assets, annotation marks and a consistent depth system. Use paper/ink colors plus [modern accent]. Animate cutouts, masks, registration and parallax with controlled imperfection. Clearly label illustrative reconstructions. No fabricated historical evidence.”

## 6. Premium 3D technology
“Show [object/system] with stable geometry and material identity. Begin at [macro detail], reveal [functional feature] through [camera path], and settle into [hero state]. Specify lights, reflections, roughness, scale and depth of field. Render a clean plate and composite exact labels later. No drifting logos or invented claims.”

## 7. Character-led explainer
“Demonstrate [concept] through an original [character/shape metaphor]. Keep silhouette, rig and prop scale consistent. Use anticipation, a clear action pose, follow-through and restrained secondary motion. Hold on the explanatory pose. Tie sound to meaningful gestures; avoid recognizable copied characters.”

## 8. Swiss / modular information design
“Organize [topic] on a strict grid with [base color] and semantic accents. Reveal modules through alignment, connection and reconfiguration rather than random flying cards. Keep labels concise and consistent. Make the grid evolve with the logic, not repeat the same layout every scene.”

## 9. Generative data field
“Visualize [relationship] through deterministic rule [rule]. Begin sparse, increase complexity with [narrative factor], then simplify to reveal [insight]. Use seeded procedural movement, [palette] and stable focal anchors. Sound follows meaningful state changes. No unseeded randomness or complexity without insight.”

## 10. Geographic / historical timeline
“Anchor the story in a sourced map or timeline. Trace [route/event], label dates and places, preserve orientation during camera movement, and use match cuts between place, artifact and year. Mark disputed geography and uncertainty. No unsupported borders or movement that implies false causality.”

## 11. Social-native vertical
“Open immediately on [visual contradiction/result]. Use one dominant subject, oversized type, strong separation and platform-safe zones. Alternate active reveals and short holds. Captions stay readable muted and never collide with UI. Keep effects purposeful and end with a real payoff.”

## 12. Live action with annotations
“Use [licensed/user-provided footage] as evidence. Add tracking-aware callouts, masks, labels, route overlays or a match cut to a simplified diagram. Preserve real context and registration. Grade to the actual footage. Do not invent measurement marks or alter factual meaning.”

## 13. Minimal premium typography
“Express [theme] through negative space, [display archetype], precise positional/scale changes and [two-tone plus accent] palette. Let a few carefully timed transitions carry emphasis; reserve long holds. Sound is restrained and intentional. Avoid empty luxury tropes.”

## 14. Mixed-media future craft
“Blend [photographic/3D medium] with [handmade/vector medium] using a repeatable visual rule. Define which parts are factual realism and which are diagram/metaphor. Use tactile but controlled texture, [specific palette] and one transition that explains the concept. AI images remain plates, never the source of exact type or facts.”

## Controlled modifiers
- More physical: specify material, weight, inertia, acceleration, collision and settle.
- More editorial: specify grid, hierarchy, crop, contrast and evidence labels.
- More cinematic: specify lens feel, motivated camera path, lighting direction, atmosphere and end frame.
- More playful: define silhouette, elastic range, rhythm, gesture and color roles.
- More restrained: reduce effects, widen negative space and lengthen holds.
- More data-dense: progressive reveals, source/units, stronger label hierarchy and longer reading time.
- More accessible: larger type, stronger contrast, slower reveals and less overlap.
- More Persian/RTL: test connected glyph shaping, numbers, punctuation, mixed-script runs and line breaks in rendered frames.

## Anti-pattern guardrail
“No generic purple gradient, purposeless glass cards, tiny labels, random camera spin, perpetual zoom, repeated whoosh per cut, incorrect text baked into generated images, unstable subject identity, unlicensed logo/character, unsourced statistic, abrupt audio jumps, unreadable captions, or unmotivated clutter.”
