# Cinematic Motion Design

## Workflow
1. Establish the audience, message, platform, duration, aspect ratio, frame rate, and delivery format.
2. Write a one-sentence creative premise and choose 3–5 visual keywords. Avoid style soup.
3. Build a beat sheet: hook, setup, escalation, reveal, resolution, end card. Assign a purpose to every shot.
4. Make a storyboard with shot duration, subject, camera, motion, typography, audio cue, and transition.
5. Define a visual system: palette, type hierarchy, grid, contrast, texture, and motion principles.
6. Animate with clear anticipation, readable action, controlled easing, and intentional holds.
7. Review at full speed and at reduced speed; remove motion that does not support the message.
8. Check legibility, safe areas, contrast, visual continuity, and export quality.

## Cinematic principles
- Prioritize silhouette and focal hierarchy before detail.
- Use motivated camera movement; every move should reveal, follow, or emphasize something.
- Vary shot scale and motion energy. Do not make every shot a push-in.
- Use negative space to let important moments land.
- Reserve the strongest contrast, scale, and sound hit for the key reveal.
- Use motion blur, grain, bloom, chromatic aberration, and lens effects sparingly and consistently.
- Design transitions around shape, direction, light, sound, or meaning rather than random effects.

## Deliverables
Return a concept, shot list, timing plan, visual-system notes, asset list, and production plan before implementation when the brief is complex.
