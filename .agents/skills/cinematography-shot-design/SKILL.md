# Cinematography and Shot Design

For each shot specify:
- Narrative purpose and subject
- Shot size and angle
- Lens/FOV and depth-of-field intent
- Camera movement, direction, speed, and end position
- Subject action and blocking
- Key/fill/rim or motivated practical lighting
- Color and contrast intent
- Duration, transition, and sound cue

## Rules
- Give each shot one dominant visual idea.
- Keep screen direction and spatial geography consistent across cuts.
- Use wide shots to establish, medium shots to explain action, and close shots to emphasize detail or emotion.
- Avoid impossible camera paths, abrupt lens changes, and unmotivated motion unless stylization is intended.
- Define the first and last frame of complex moves to improve consistency.
- For text-to-video prompts, describe observable actions and camera behavior concretely; avoid contradictory instructions.
- Use reference frames or a shot bible to maintain subject, wardrobe, palette, and location continuity.
