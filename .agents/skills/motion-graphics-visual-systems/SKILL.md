# Motion Graphics Visual Systems

## Visual bible
Define audience and emotion; 3-5 core colors plus semantic colors; display, body, and mono type roles; grid and safe zones; material language; lighting and shadow rules; camera/lens vocabulary; texture policy; transition family; motion curves; and whitespace/density rules.

## Visual modes
Choose based on the story:
1. expressive kinetic typography and word-level emphasis;
2. modular vector illustration and shape morphing;
3. exploded diagrams and technical cutaways;
4. animated charts, axes, labels, and uncertainty;
5. maps, routes, timelines, and geographic scale;
6. editorial collage and layered cutouts;
7. 2.5D parallax with controlled depth;
8. procedural patterns, particles, fields, and generative geometry;
9. true 3D objects or environments with deliberate camera movement;
10. interfaces and product walkthroughs;
11. macro material details and slow reveals;
12. miniature worlds and visual metaphors;
13. sourced archival imagery with restrained annotations;
14. split-screen comparisons and before/after;
15. diagrams transforming into concrete scenes.

## Composition
Give each frame one dominant focal point and a clear reading order. Use scale, contrast, position, direction, and timing for hierarchy. Balance visual mass and negative space. Keep important text away from platform UI. Use depth to separate planes, not as decoration. Reuse motifs as a visual grammar, not repetitive templates.

## Motion grammar
Use ease-out for arrivals, ease-in for departures, spring/overshoot for playful energy, linear movement for data travel or machinery, and custom curves for hero moments. Stagger by hierarchy. Keep a readable hold after kinetic type resolves. Avoid animating every element at once.

## Motivated transitions
Use match cuts, shape wipes, object occlusion, camera-through geometry, line tracing, spatial pushes, data-to-object morphs, or typography masks when the ideas connect. Build reusable transition primitives.

## Implementation mapping
Exact text and charts: Remotion/DOM/SVG/Canvas. Procedural vector: SVG/GSAP/CSS/Canvas. True 3D: Blender or Three.js. AI footage: short inserts with continuity anchors; composite exact titles separately. Keep frame output deterministic and avoid wall-clock randomness.
## Acceptance
Every major motion has a narrative or explanatory purpose. No clipping, inconsistent typography, accidental palette shifts, unreadable labels, or repetitive template sequences.
## Status
Original guide; not a bundled third-party dependency.
